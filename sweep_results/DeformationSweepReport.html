What was tested

Two synthetic airways with 6 generations (trachea = generation 0) and 45° bifurcations: a planar tree (55 skeleton branches, every bifurcation in one plane) and a non-planar tree whose bifurcation plane turns 45° about each child branch (63 branches). Each tree was deformed 31 ways, from mild to severe, in six families: translation, rotation about an oblique axis, rotation plus translation, rotation plus translation plus shear, a random smooth B-spline warp, and a breath hold that stretches the nonterminal tree craniocaudally (most at the bases) and expands it laterally while carrying each terminal branch rigidly, as between two CT breath holds.

Every moving mask was scored by the AirwayEvaluation CLI with --deformableEvaluation under all 135 combinations of the four registration hyperparameters below, and once without registration as the starting point. TLD is tree length detected and BD branch detection, both from the CLI's results.csv. A deformation passes when TLD ≥ 95% and every generation has 100% BD. CLI errors (fewer than 4 landmark pairs for the thin-plate spline) count as TLD 0.

Findings

The defaults leave most of the recoverable tree unrecovered

On the non-planar tree the CLI defaults average 87.4% TLD and pass 4 of 31 deformations; they rank 97th of 135 settings. The recommended setting averages 98.1%, passes 31 of 31, never errors, and its worst deformation still reaches 96.7% TLD. On the planar tree it lifts mean TLD from 81.1% to 96.1% and passes from 8 to 23.

Maximum pairing distance is the parameter that matters

Averaged over everything else, non-planar mean TLD rises from 75.3% at 2.5 to 88.6% at 5, 93.4% at 10, 95.0% at 20 and 95.2% at 40, and the CLI error rate falls from 18% at 2.5 to 2% at 5 and 0 from 10 up. The rigid (ICP) alignment cannot absorb nonrigid motion, so a tight cutoff drops correct landmark pairs; the spline is then fitted to a few points, or to fewer than 4 and the run errors.

Keep nearby branch points as separate landmarks

A minimum landmark distance of 2 gives 92.7% mean TLD on the non-planar tree against 87.9% at 5 or 10, which give identical results on these phantoms. More landmarks give the spline more control points along the tree.

Sensitivity and projection neighbors do not change the result here

Sensitivity 1, 2 and 3 give identical results on both trees, and 1, 5 or 15 projection neighbors move mean TLD by at most 0.3 points. The phantoms have no spurious short leaves for sensitivity to prune, so this sweep says nothing about its value on real segmentations.

Every tested deformation of the non-planar tree is recovered

With the recommended setting the non-planar tree passes up to a 60-voxel translation, a 45° rotation, 30° plus 40 voxels, shear 0.2, a 16-voxel B-spline warp and a 30% breath hold (37 voxels at the bases), with 100% branch detection in every generation. With the defaults, no rotation, combined motion or B-spline case passes, and only the 2% breath hold does. The breaking point lies beyond the tested range.

One setting captures nearly all of what is recoverable

The best setting chosen separately for each deformation beats the recommended single setting by at most 0.1 points of mean TLD per family on the non-planar tree, so there is little to gain from tuning per deformation type.

The planar tree stays harder

With every landmark in one plane the spline's affine part is undetermined. Planar rotations recover to 90.6% mean TLD (3 of 5 pass, worst 65.1% at 30°), and on the 30° + 40-voxel case the recommended setting does worse than the defaults (70.7% vs 92.3% TLD). A pairing distance of 20 is marginally better on this tree (96.5% mean, worst 71.3%).

Where the remaining loss is

With the defaults, the losses sit in the distal generations: on the non-planar tree generations 4 and 5 average about 93% branch detection across the deformations, while the trachea and first generations stay near 97–100%. With the recommended setting every generation of the non-planar tree reaches 100% on every deformation. The planar tree keeps a small residual loss spread over generations 1–5, from its large rotations.

What each hyperparameter does

Pairing distance dominates: raising it from the default 5 to 20 or 40 adds 6–7 points of mean TLD on the non-planar tree and 10–11 on the planar tree and removes every CLI error. A minimum landmark distance of 2 adds about 5 points. Sensitivity and projection neighbors are flat. The pass-rate panels move less than mean TLD because each point averages settings whose other parameters are poor.

--branchDetectionThresholdPercent changes only how a branch is counted, not the registration. Scoring the same default-parameter registrations with a 25%, 50% or 75% coverage threshold moves mean BD by 8–16 points for the nonrigid and breath-hold families. It was held at 50% for every result above and should be reported alongside any BD figure, not tuned.

Recommended parameters

For the best recovery through deformation, run the CLI with:

AirwayEvaluation --deformableEvaluation --maximumPairingDistance 40 --minimumLandmarkDistance 2 --sensitivityMultiplier 2 --projectionNeighbors 5
ParameterCLI defaultRecommended
Max pairing distance540
Min landmark distance52
Sensitivity multiplier22
Projection neighbors55

Why 40 rather than 20. On the non-planar tree, 40 passes 31 of 31 deformations against 30 for 20, with a higher worst case (96.7% vs 94.6% TLD). On the planar tree, 20 is slightly better (96.5% vs 96.1% mean TLD). Both are far ahead of the default of 5, and above 20 the gain is small.

Check before adopting 40 on real data. Both phantoms here have the same branches as their deformed copies. In the one-to-one pairing, any two leftover landmarks closer than the cutoff are cheaper to pair than to leave unpaired, so with a 40 mm cutoff a prediction with missing or extra branches can have a spurious branch point paired with a real one up to 40 mm away. Sweep a prediction with pruned or added branches before adopting 40. Until then, --maximumPairingDistance 20 keeps nearly all of the gain with half the reach.

Leave sensitivity at 2 and projection neighbors at 5. They had no measurable effect on these phantoms, and sensitivity controls pruning of spurious leaves in real segmentations, which this sweep does not exercise.
